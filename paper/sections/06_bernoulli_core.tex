\section{Small Patches under Common-Uniform Bernoulli Replay}
\label{sec:bernoulli}

The Gaussian model makes the graph allocation exact, but a bounded model exposes
a stronger regression phenomenon.  Consider binary software outcomes represented
as threshold functions of a replayable uniform random tape:
\[
Y_j(v)=\mathbf 1\{U\le p_{j,v}\},
\qquad U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1].
\]
This is a monotone coupling of Bernoulli marginals.

\begin{proposition}[Sparse differential uncertainty]
\label{prop:bernoulli}
Let $d_v=|p_{1,v}-p_{0,v}|$.  Under common-uniform replay,
\[
\E[Y_1(v)-Y_0(v)]=p_{1,v}-p_{0,v}
\]
and
\[
\mathrm{Var}(Y_1(v)-Y_0(v))=d_v(1-d_v).
\]
Under independent old/new replay, the difference has variance
\[
p_{0,v}(1-p_{0,v})+p_{1,v}(1-p_{1,v}).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $p_{1,v}\ge p_{0,v}$, the paired difference equals one exactly when
$U\in(p_{0,v},p_{1,v}]$, an event of probability $d_v$, and is otherwise zero.
Thus it is Bernoulli$(d_v)$.  If the ordering is reversed, the difference is the
negative of a Bernoulli$(d_v)$ variable, with the same variance.  The independent
formula follows by adding the two independent Bernoulli variances.
\end{proof}

If $p_{0,v}$ stays in the interior of $(0,1)$ while $d_v\to0$, the paired variance
is $\Theta(d_v)$ whereas the independent-replay variance is $\Theta(1)$.  The
variance observation by itself is classical in spirit: pairing and common random
numbers are established variance-reduction ideas \cite{GlassermanYao1992}, and
paired binary outcomes have a long statistical literature \cite{Connor1987}.
The regression question is whether executable old/new coupling changes the
\emph{query complexity} of detecting a software fairness regression.

\subsection{A linear-versus-quadratic small-patch separation}

Consider one counterfactual edge $e=(u,v)$ with threshold $\tau_e=0$.  Fix
$p\in(0,1)$ and $d\in(0,1-p)$.  In both hypotheses the old version is perfectly
fair:
\[
p_{0,u}=p_{0,v}=p.
\]
Under the null the patch changes neither endpoint,
\[
\mathsf H_0:
\quad
p_{1,u}=p_{1,v}=p,
\]
whereas under the alternative it changes only $u$,
\[
\mathsf H_1:
\quad
p_{1,u}=p+d,
\qquad
p_{1,v}=p.
\]
Thus $\mathsf H_1$ introduces a fairness regression of magnitude exactly $d$.

\begin{theorem}[Replay changes small-patch complexity]
\label{thm:bernoulli-separation}
Fix $p\in(0,1)$ and $\delta\in(0,1/2)$.  For the one-edge family above:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Under common-uniform paired replay, there is a test using
\[
n_{\rm pair}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{\log \delta}{\log(1-d)}
\right\rceil
\]
paired rounds with zero type-I error and type-II error at most $\delta$.
Moreover, every adaptive $\delta$-correct paired-replay tester with finite
expected stopping time satisfies, on the null,
\[
\E_0[T]
\ge
\frac{\kl(1-\delta,\delta)}
{(1-p)\log\!\left(\frac{1-p}{1-p-d}\right)}.
\]

\item Under independent/fresh replay, a test using
\[
n_{\rm ind}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{2\log(1/\delta)}{d^2}
\right\rceil
\]
new-version samples at $u$ is $\delta$-correct when the retained old baseline
$p$ is known.  Conversely, every adaptive $\delta$-correct independent-replay
tester with finite expected stopping time satisfies
\[
\E_0[T]
\ge
\frac{\kl(1-\delta,\delta)}
{\kl(p,p+d)}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

Consequently, for every fixed interior $p$,
\[
Q_{\rm pair}(d,\delta)
=
\Theta\!\left(
\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{d}
\right),
\qquad
Q_{\rm ind}(d,\delta)
=
\Theta\!\left(
\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{d^2}
\right)
\]
as $d\to0$, up to constants depending on $p$ and the standard
$\kl(1-\delta,\delta)$ versus $\log(1/\delta)$ equivalence.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For paired replay, run $P_0$ and $P_1$ on $u$ using the same
$U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$ and reject upon observing a discordant pair
$(Y_0,Y_1)=(0,1)$.  Under $\mathsf H_0$ discordance is impossible.  Under
$\mathsf H_1$ it occurs exactly when $U\in(p,p+d]$, hence independently with
probability $d$ each round.  The miss probability after $n$ rounds is therefore
$(1-d)^n$, proving the upper bound.

For the paired lower bound, one informative query at $u$ has null law
\[
Q_0(1,1)=p,
\qquad
Q_0(0,0)=1-p,
\]
and alternative law
\[
Q_1(1,1)=p,
\qquad
Q_1(0,0)=1-p-d,
\qquad
Q_1(0,1)=d.
\]
Thus
\[
\KL(Q_0\|Q_1)
=
(1-p)\log\!\left(\frac{1-p}{1-p-d}\right).
\]
Queries to $v$ have identical laws in both hypotheses.  The adaptive transcript
chain rule and data processing through the terminal accept/reject decision give
\[
\E_0[N_u]\KL(Q_0\|Q_1)
\ge
\kl(1-\delta,\delta),
\]
which yields the stated lower bound.

For independent replay, giving the tester the exact old baseline $p$ can only make
the problem easier.  Sampling the new version at $u$ and rejecting when its
empirical mean is at least $p+d/2$ has each one-sided error bounded by
$\exp(-nd^2/2)$ by Hoeffding's inequality, proving the upper bound.
For the lower bound, the old-version observation has the same distribution under
both hypotheses and contributes no information.  Each informative new-version
sample contributes
\[
\KL(\mathrm{Bern}(p)\|\mathrm{Bern}(p+d))
=
\kl(p,p+d).
\]
The same transcript change-of-measure argument yields the claimed expected-sample
lower bound.

Finally,
\[
(1-p)\log\!\left(\frac{1-p}{1-p-d}\right)
=
d+O(d^2),
\]
whereas
\[
\kl(p,p+d)
=
\frac{d^2}{2p(1-p)}+O(d^3).
\]
The asymptotic separation follows.
\end{proof}

\Cref{thm:bernoulli-separation} is deliberately stated on the smallest possible
fairness graph.  Its role is to isolate a version-specific information advantage
that cannot be attributed to graph allocation: replayability changes the
small-patch exponent from two to one.  The next target is to lift this phenomenon
to graph-structured fairness regression, where sparse patch deltas and shared
vertices interact.


\subsection{Four-way counterfactual replay cancels common patch effects}

The one-edge separation above uses a null in which the patch has no effect.  We
next show that a fairness-specific replay design preserves the linear small-patch
rate even when both counterfactual inputs undergo a nonzero common change.

Consider one edge \(e=(u,v)\).  Fix
\[
p>0,\qquad s>0,\qquad d>0,\qquad p+s+d<1.
\]
Both hypotheses use the same fair old version,
\[
p_{0,u}=p_{0,v}=p.
\]
Under the null, the patch changes both endpoints equally:
\[
\mathsf H_0:
\qquad
p_{1,u}=p_{1,v}=p+s.
\]
Under the alternative, \(u\) receives an additional differential effect \(d\):
\[
\mathsf H_1:
\qquad
p_{1,u}=p+s+d,
\qquad
p_{1,v}=p+s.
\]
Thus the common patch effect \(s\) is fairness-neutral, while \(d\) is precisely
the introduced fairness gap.

A \emph{vertex-paired} call shares a random tape between \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) at
one endpoint.  A \emph{four-way counterfactual replay} call goes further: the same
\(U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]\) is reused across both versions and both endpoints,
requiring four concrete program executions.

\begin{theorem}[Counterfactual replay changes the small-gap exponent]
\label{thm:counterfactual-replay}
Fix \(p,s\) as above and \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\).

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Under four-way counterfactual replay, there is a test using
\[
n_{\rm edge}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{\log\delta}{\log(1-d)}
\right\rceil
\]
replay rounds with zero type-I error and type-II error at most \(\delta\).
Every adaptive \(\delta\)-correct tester with finite expected stopping time
satisfies, on the null,
\[
\E_0[T]
\ge
\frac{\kl(1-\delta,\delta)}
{r\log\!\left(\frac{r}{r-d}\right)},
\qquad
r=1-p-s.
\]

\item Under vertex-paired replay, even granting the tester \(p\), \(s\), and the
identity of the endpoint that may receive the differential effect, a
\(\delta\)-correct test exists using
\[
n_{\rm vertex}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{2\log(1/\delta)}{d^2}
\right\rceil
\]
paired-oracle calls.  Conversely, every adaptive \(\delta\)-correct
vertex-paired tester with finite expected stopping time satisfies
\[
\E_0[T]
\ge
\frac{\kl(1-\delta,\delta)}
{
s\log\!\left(\frac{s}{s+d}\right)
+
r\log\!\left(\frac{r}{r-d}\right)
}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

Consequently, for fixed \(p,s,r>0\),
\[
Q_{\rm edge}(d,\delta)
=
\Theta\!\left(
\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{d}
\right),
\qquad
Q_{\rm vertex}(d,\delta)
=
\Theta\!\left(
\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{d^2}
\right)
\]
as \(d\to0\), up to constant factors.  Counting concrete program executions
multiplies the two costs by four and two, respectively, and therefore does not
change the exponent separation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under four-way replay, define the pathwise differential
\[
W
=
\bigl(P_1(u;U)-P_0(u;U)\bigr)
-
\bigl(P_1(v;U)-P_0(v;U)\bigr).
\]
Under \(\mathsf H_0\), the two endpoint deltas are identical for every \(U\), so
\(W=0\) almost surely.  Under \(\mathsf H_1\),
\[
W=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
U\in(p+s,p+s+d],
\]
which occurs with probability \(d\).  Rejecting upon the first \(W=1\) therefore
has zero type-I error and miss probability \((1-d)^n\), proving the upper bound.

For the lower bound, the four-output observation has the same mass \(p\) on the
all-one state and the same mass \(s\) on the state in which both new-version
outputs are one while both old-version outputs are zero.  Under the null, the
remaining all-zero state has mass \(r=1-p-s\).  Under the alternative, mass \(d\)
is split from that state into a new state witnessing the counterfactual
differential.  Hence the null-to-alternative KL of one four-way round is
\[
K_{\rm edge}
=
r\log\!\left(\frac{r}{r-d}\right).
\]
Adaptive transcript change of measure followed by data processing through the
terminal decision gives
\[
\E_0[T]K_{\rm edge}
\ge
\kl(1-\delta,\delta).
\]

For vertex-paired replay, querying the potentially changed endpoint \(u\) produces
the pair distribution
\[
(p,\ s,\ r)
\]
on states \((1,1),(0,1),(0,0)\) under the null, and
\[
(p,\ s+d,\ r-d)
\]
under the alternative.  Its KL is therefore
\[
K_{\rm vertex}
=
s\log\!\left(\frac{s}{s+d}\right)
+
r\log\!\left(\frac{r}{r-d}\right).
\]
Queries to \(v\) carry no information, so the same transcript argument proves the
lower bound.  For the upper bound, ignore the extra pair information and retain
only the discordance indicator.  It is Bernoulli\((s)\) under the null and
Bernoulli\((s+d)\) under the alternative.  Thresholding its empirical mean at
\(s+d/2\) and applying Hoeffding's inequality bounds each one-sided error by
\(\exp(-nd^2/2)\).

Finally,
\[
K_{\rm edge}
=
d+O(d^2),
\]
while the first-order terms in \(K_{\rm vertex}\) cancel and
\[
K_{\rm vertex}
=
\frac{d^2}{2}
\left(
\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{r}
\right)
+
O(d^3).
\]
The claimed asymptotic rates follow.
\end{proof}

The mechanism is a specialized use of common randomness, not a claim that common
random numbers or probabilistic couplings are new
\cite{GlassermanYao1992,BartheEtAl2017}.  The result concerns the
fairness-regression access model: sharing randomness across \emph{both} software
versions and \emph{both} counterfactual inputs makes fairness-neutral patch
behavior cancel pathwise, changing the information rate for the differential
fairness effect.
