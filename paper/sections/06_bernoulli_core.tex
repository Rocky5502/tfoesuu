\section{Small Patches under Common-Uniform Bernoulli Replay}
\label{sec:bernoulli}

The Gaussian model makes the graph allocation exact, but a bounded model exposes
a stronger regression phenomenon.  Consider binary software outcomes represented
as threshold functions of a replayable uniform random tape:
\[
Y_j(v)=\mathbf 1\{U\le p_{j,v}\},
\qquad U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1].
\]
This is a monotone coupling of Bernoulli marginals.

\begin{proposition}[Sparse differential uncertainty]
\label{prop:bernoulli}
Let $d_v=|p_{1,v}-p_{0,v}|$.  Under common-uniform replay,
\[
\E[Y_1(v)-Y_0(v)]=p_{1,v}-p_{0,v}
\]
and
\[
\mathrm{Var}(Y_1(v)-Y_0(v))=d_v(1-d_v).
\]
Under independent old/new replay, the difference has variance
\[
p_{0,v}(1-p_{0,v})+p_{1,v}(1-p_{1,v}).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $p_{1,v}\ge p_{0,v}$, the paired difference equals one exactly when
$U\in(p_{0,v},p_{1,v}]$, an event of probability $d_v$, and is otherwise zero.
Thus it is Bernoulli$(d_v)$.  If the ordering is reversed, the difference is the
negative of a Bernoulli$(d_v)$ variable, with the same variance.  The independent
formula follows by adding the two independent Bernoulli variances.
\end{proof}

If $p_{0,v}$ stays in the interior of $(0,1)$ while $d_v\to0$, the paired variance
is $\Theta(d_v)$ whereas the independent-replay variance is $\Theta(1)$.  The
variance observation by itself is classical in spirit: pairing and common random
numbers are established variance-reduction ideas \cite{GlassermanYao1992}, and
paired binary outcomes have a long statistical literature \cite{Connor1987}.
The regression question is whether executable old/new coupling changes the
\emph{query complexity} of detecting a software fairness regression.

\subsection{A linear-versus-quadratic small-patch separation}

Consider one counterfactual edge $e=(u,v)$ with threshold $\tau_e=0$.  Fix
$p\in(0,1)$ and $d\in(0,1-p)$.  In both hypotheses the old version is perfectly
fair:
\[
p_{0,u}=p_{0,v}=p.
\]
Under the null the patch changes neither endpoint,
\[
\mathsf H_0:
\quad
p_{1,u}=p_{1,v}=p,
\]
whereas under the alternative it changes only $u$,
\[
\mathsf H_1:
\quad
p_{1,u}=p+d,
\qquad
p_{1,v}=p.
\]
Thus $\mathsf H_1$ introduces a fairness regression of magnitude exactly $d$.

\begin{theorem}[Replay changes small-patch complexity]
\label{thm:bernoulli-separation}
Fix $p\in(0,1)$ and $\delta\in(0,1/2)$.  For the one-edge family above:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Under common-uniform paired replay, there is a test using
\[
n_{\rm pair}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{\log \delta}{\log(1-d)}
\right\rceil
\]
paired rounds with zero type-I error and type-II error at most $\delta$.
Moreover, every adaptive $\delta$-correct paired-replay tester with finite
expected stopping time satisfies, on the null,
\[
\E_0[T]
\ge
\frac{\kl(1-\delta,\delta)}
{(1-p)\log\!\left(\frac{1-p}{1-p-d}\right)}.
\]

\item Under independent/fresh replay, a test using
\[
n_{\rm ind}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{2\log(1/\delta)}{d^2}
\right\rceil
\]
new-version samples at $u$ is $\delta$-correct when the retained old baseline
$p$ is known.  Conversely, every adaptive $\delta$-correct independent-replay
tester with finite expected stopping time satisfies
\[
\E_0[T]
\ge
\frac{\kl(1-\delta,\delta)}
{\kl(p,p+d)}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

Consequently, for every fixed interior $p$,
\[
Q_{\rm pair}(d,\delta)
=
\Theta\!\left(
\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{d}
\right),
\qquad
Q_{\rm ind}(d,\delta)
=
\Theta\!\left(
\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{d^2}
\right)
\]
as $d\to0$, up to constants depending on $p$ and the standard
$\kl(1-\delta,\delta)$ versus $\log(1/\delta)$ equivalence.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For paired replay, run $P_0$ and $P_1$ on $u$ using the same
$U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$ and reject upon observing a discordant pair
$(Y_0,Y_1)=(0,1)$.  Under $\mathsf H_0$ discordance is impossible.  Under
$\mathsf H_1$ it occurs exactly when $U\in(p,p+d]$, hence independently with
probability $d$ each round.  The miss probability after $n$ rounds is therefore
$(1-d)^n$, proving the upper bound.

For the paired lower bound, one informative query at $u$ has null law
\[
Q_0(1,1)=p,
\qquad
Q_0(0,0)=1-p,
\]
and alternative law
\[
Q_1(1,1)=p,
\qquad
Q_1(0,0)=1-p-d,
\qquad
Q_1(0,1)=d.
\]
Thus
\[
\KL(Q_0\|Q_1)
=
(1-p)\log\!\left(\frac{1-p}{1-p-d}\right).
\]
Queries to $v$ have identical laws in both hypotheses.  The adaptive transcript
chain rule and data processing through the terminal accept/reject decision give
\[
\E_0[N_u]\KL(Q_0\|Q_1)
\ge
\kl(1-\delta,\delta),
\]
which yields the stated lower bound.

For independent replay, giving the tester the exact old baseline $p$ can only make
the problem easier.  Sampling the new version at $u$ and rejecting when its
empirical mean is at least $p+d/2$ has each one-sided error bounded by
$\exp(-nd^2/2)$ by Hoeffding's inequality, proving the upper bound.
For the lower bound, the old-version observation has the same distribution under
both hypotheses and contributes no information.  Each informative new-version
sample contributes
\[
\KL(\mathrm{Bern}(p)\|\mathrm{Bern}(p+d))
=
\kl(p,p+d).
\]
The same transcript change-of-measure argument yields the claimed expected-sample
lower bound.

Finally,
\[
(1-p)\log\!\left(\frac{1-p}{1-p-d}\right)
=
d+O(d^2),
\]
whereas
\[
\kl(p,p+d)
=
\frac{d^2}{2p(1-p)}+O(d^3).
\]
The asymptotic separation follows.
\end{proof}

\Cref{thm:bernoulli-separation} is deliberately stated on the smallest possible
fairness graph.  Its role is to isolate a version-specific information advantage
that cannot be attributed to graph allocation: replayability changes the
small-patch exponent from two to one.  The next target is to lift this phenomenon
to graph-structured fairness regression, where sparse patch deltas and shared
vertices interact.
