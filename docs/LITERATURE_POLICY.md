# Literature and citation policy

The paper should cite the strongest archival source available.

## Preferred source order

1. peer-reviewed top conference or journal paper;
2. peer-reviewed archival journal version of an earlier conference/preprint;
3. canonical conference paper when it is the field-standard source;
4. preprint only when no archival version exists and the work is essential.

The core bibliography should avoid weak or convenience-only preprints. Citation counts are not used as a substitute for venue quality or relevance.

## Required novelty boundaries

We do **not** claim to invent:

- fairness testing of software;
- regression testing or change-impact analysis;
- active/query-efficient fairness auditing;
- property testing;
- common random numbers/coupling as a general variance-reduction technique;
- generic graph-based optimal design for pairwise mean differences;
- incremental verification.

The intended novelty must come from the evolving-software fairness problem and its version-aware execution/query complexity.

## Core archival anchors

The manuscript currently prioritizes peer-reviewed sources from ESEC/FSE, IEEE TSE, Software Testing Verification and Reliability, STOC, ICML, POPL, ISSTA/PACMSE, OOPSLA/PACMPL, Management Science, Scandinavian Journal of Statistics, Theory of Computing, Computational Complexity, and ISAAC/Springer where directly relevant.

Every DOI and venue entry must be checked against the publisher page before submission.
